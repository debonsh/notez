Lesson · BT201 Unit 1 — Quantum Mechanics

Wave Nature & de Broglie Hypothesis — Matter Has a Wavelength

Classical mechanics explains planets and cricket balls. It fails for electrons, photons, and atoms. Light shows interference (wave) AND photoelectric effect (particle). De Broglie (1924) flipped it: if waves act like particles, particles act like waves. RGPV asks the derivation + one numeral, every cycle.

Why quantum was needed

Classical physics could not explain: black-body radiation, photoelectric effect (Einstein, 1905), Compton scattering, stability of atoms. All need quantization: energy comes in packets E = hf.

EvidenceShows
Interference, diffraction, polarizationLight is a wave
Photoelectric effect, Compton effectLight is a particle (photon, E = hf)
Davisson–Germer (electron diffraction off Ni crystal)Electrons are waves — confirms de Broglie

de Broglie hypothesis (1924)

Statement (write verbatim): every moving material particle has a wave associated with it, with wavelength inversely proportional to its momentum:

λ = h / p = h / mv

where h = 6.626 × 10⁻³⁴ J·s, p = mv = momentum.

Derivation — λ = h/p (4 steps, RGPV favorite)

Step 1: Photon energy (Planck):      E = hf
Step 2: Mass-energy (Einstein):      E = mc²
Step 3: Equate:  hf = mc²  with  f = c/λ,  p = mc
Step 4: h(c/λ) = pc  →  λ = h/p
Exam note This derivation mixes photon + relativity to motivate the formula, then de Broglie postulates it holds for ALL matter (electrons, protons, even balls). Say that line explicitly for full marks.

Properties of matter waves

  • Not electromagnetic — no E/B field needed; they are probability waves (|ψ|²).
  • Wavelength λ decreases as momentum increases (faster/heavier → shorter λ).
  • Macroscopic objects: λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m — undetectable, so classical mechanics still works.
  • Microscopic particles: λ ~ Å (atomic spacing) — diffraction observable.
  • Phase velocity can exceed c; information travels at group velocity (Topic 05).

Electron accelerated through potential V (the numeral machine)

Electron (charge e, mass m) starts from rest, accelerated by V volts:

Step 1: KE gained = work done:   eV = p²/2m
Step 2: momentum:                p = √(2meV)
Step 3: de Broglie:              λ = h/p = h/√(2meV)
Step 4: numbers (electron):      λ = 1.227/√V  nm   (V in volts)
Constants to memorize h = 6.626×10⁻³⁴, mₚ = 9.1×10⁻³¹, e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹. Final answer in nm.

Q: Electron accelerated through 100 V. Find λ.

StepWork

3λ = 1.227/10 = 0.1227 nm ≈ 1.23 Å

Solved A — electron through 150 V (do-not-skip-a-step version)

StepWork (write exactly this)
GivenV = 150 V (electron, so shortcut allowed)
Formulaλ = 1.227/√V nm
√V√150 = √(25×6) = 5√6 ≈ 5×2.449 = 12.247
Divideλ = 1.227/12.247 ≈ 0.1002 nm
Answerλ ≈ 0.10 nm = 1.0 Å (X-ray range ✓)

Why it works: higher V → more momentum → shorter λ. 150 V > 100 V so λ must be < 0.1227 nm — 0.10 nm passes the sanity check.

Solved C — full-constant derivation (when they forbid the shortcut)

Q: Derive 1.227/√V from h, m, e (do once, then quote shortcut forever).

StepWork
1λ = h/√(2meV) = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / √(2 × 9.1×10⁻³¹ × 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ × V)
2Denominator: 2×9.1×1.6 = 29.12; powers: 10⁻³¹⁻¹⁹ = 10⁻5⁰ → √(29.12×10⁻5⁰V) = 5.396×10⁻²5·√V
3λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (5.396×10⁻²5√V) = 1.227×10⁻⁹ / √V m
410⁻⁹ m = 1 nm → λ = 1.227/√V nm ✓
Numeral traps (lose marks here)
  • Grams → kg: 1 g = 10⁻³ kg. Forgetting this shifts answer by 1000×.
  • Å ↔ nm: 1 Å = 0.1 nm = 10⁻¹⁰ m. Convert at the END only.
  • Shortcut scope: 1.227/√V is ELECTRONS ONLY. Proton/alpha particle → use h/√(2mqV) with its own m, q.
  • Square root: √(a×10b) mistakes — split into √a × 10b/2.
  • No unit = −1 mark: every line carries its unit (kg, m/s, V, nm).
Special lines for extra marks (copy these)
  • "The Davisson–Germer electron diffraction confirmed λ = h/p, establishing wave–particle duality for matter."
  • "λ ≈ 0.1 nm lies in the X-ray region, hence electrons of ~150 V resolve atomic spacings — principle of the electron microscope."
  • "For macroscopic masses λ ~ 10⁻³⁰ m is far below detection limits; hence classical mechanics remains valid at everyday scales."
  • End every derivation with "Hence proved." End every numeral with a boxed answer + unit.
Exam tips — weak-physics edition
  • Write the statement + formula FIRST (1 mark) before any algebra — even if you fumble later, you bank marks.
  • Draw a labelled wave sketch (λ marked) or Davisson–Germer setup — diagrams fetch 1–2 grace marks.
  • Underline formulas, box final answers. Examiners scan; make marks easy to give.
  • If √V is ugly (e.g. √150), show the split (5√6) — method marks even if arithmetic slips.
  • Always add one physical-significance line after the number (see special lines above).

Wave Nature & de Broglie — Quick Reference

The statement (write verbatim)

Every moving particle has a wave: λ = h/p = h/mv.

Derivation (4 steps)

1. E = hf (Planck)      2. E = mc² (Einstein)
3. hf = mc², f = c/λ, p = mc
4. λ = h/p  → postulate for all matter

Electron through V

eV = p²/2m → p = √(2meV)
λ = h/√(2meV) = 1.227/√V nm

V → λ table (electron)

V (volts)λ
1000.123 nm

10000.039 nm

Properties (MCQ gold)

  • Probability waves (|ψ|²), no medium, no E/B field
  • λ ∝ 1/p — heavier/faster → shorter λ
  • Macro λ ~ 10⁻³¹ m → classical OK
  • Confirmed by Davisson–Germer electron diffraction

Key definitions

  • Duality: light/matter show wave AND particle behavior
  • Photon: E = hf, p = h/λ
  • Matter wave: λ = h/mv for any moving particle
