BT201 Mid-Sem — Predicted Paper & Battle Plan

Units 1 and 2 — Quantum Mechanics and Wave Optics. Two facts reshape how you revise this. First, the marks in BT-201 are spread almost evenly across all five units (18–23% each), so no unit can be dropped. Second, unlike BT-102, this subject is roughly 80% derivation and 20% numerical, and the numericals all come from just two places: Newton's rings and optical-fibre numerical aperture. Section 1 · What will actually be on the paper

Format, and the assumption that keeps getting made

Read this before you plan the paper

Every RGPV first-year end-sem paper says the same three things: “Attempt any five questions. All questions carry equal marks. In case of any doubt the English version is final.” It prints 8 questions for 70 marks, so each is 14 marks, normally (a) 7 + (b) 7. There is no Section A / Section B and no compulsory-versus-choice split.

Several exam-prep pages claim RGPV papers run as “Section A: 5 × 2 marks, Section B: 2 × 5 marks”. That format is not in any RGPV paper. It was checked against 7 real scans across seven papers (Nov-2019 to Dec-2025).

What is actually known about the mid-sem itself

Confirmed from the official RGPV ordinance and the July 2018 scheme: the whole semester mid-sem bucket is 20 marks per theory subject (70 end-sem + 20 mid-sem + 10 internal = 100), and RGPV requires at least two mid-term tests. So MST-1 is most likely 10 marks.

Two things nobody can confirm, because RGPV publishes no mid-sem papers and the Board of Studies sets the detail locally: whether MST-1 is 10 or 20 marks, and the paper's duration and section layout.

One real data point survives: an ITM Gwalior BT-204 Mid Term 1 paper circulates online and its instruction line reads “Attempt all questions.” No choice at all — a small question count, all compulsory, equal marks, which is the end-sem pattern scaled down.

How to plan for a paper nobody has seen

Do not memorise a layout. Memorise the topics — they transfer between any layout. Then when the paper lands, read the marks against each question and spend your time proportionally. The predicted paper below is written in the end-sem format because that is the house style RGPV reuses.

(a) State and prove the Heisenberg uncertainty principle with its elementary proof.

(b) Derive the time-dependent and time-independent Schrödinger equations.

Why: Uncertainty 6 of 7 papers, Schr&ouml;dinger 6 of 7. The two most-asked Unit 1 stems, and both in your mid-sem scope. Prep: unit1/03-schrodinger-equation.html (not built yet)

Q2 · U1 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Define the wave function, state its physical significance and the conditions it must satisfy.

(b) Deduce the energy of a particle trapped in a one-dimensional box, and write its wave function.

Why: Wave function significance 5 of 7, energy in a box 4 of 7. Dec-2025 set the energy question as a numerical with m = 9.11&times;10<sup>&minus;31</sup> kg and L = 10 nm. Prep: unit1/04-particle-in-a-box.html (not built yet)

Q3 · U1 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Establish the relation between phase velocity and group velocity.

(b) Discuss the energy and momentum operators.

Why: vg/vp relation 4 of 7, operators 5 of 7. The v<sub>g</sub> = 2v<sub>p</sub> result for a free particle is the mark that distinguishes a full answer from a restatement of definitions. Prep: unit1/02-wave-nature-debroglie.html (not built yet)

Q4 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Explain Young's double-slit experiment and derive the expression for fringe width.

(b) Explain Fraunhofer diffraction from a single slit.

Why: YDSE 4 of 7, single slit 3 of 7. The Unit 2 pair that most often appears together. Prep: unit2/01-youngs-double-slit.html (not built yet)

Q5 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Explain the formation of Newton's rings and derive the expression for the diameter of dark and bright fringes.

(b) Given the diameters of the nth and (n+1)th dark rings and the radius of curvature, calculate the wavelength of light.

Why: Newton's rings in 6 of 7 papers &mdash; the single most reliable Unit 2 question, and it arrives with a numerical attached more often than not. Prep: unit2/02-newtons-rings.html (not built yet)

Q6 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Explain the construction and working of the Michelson interferometer, with its types of fringes.

(b) Explain the Rayleigh criterion of resolution and the resolving power of a diffraction grating.

Why: Michelson 5 of 7, Rayleigh criterion 4 of 7. Both are pure explanation, no derivation &mdash; they are marks for structure, not algebra. Prep: unit2/03-michelson-interferometer.html (not built yet)

Q7 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) A plane transmission grating has 4250 lines per cm and a second-order spectral line is observed at 30°. Find the wavelength.

(b) In Newton's rings in reflected light, the diameter of the 10th dark ring is 0.50 cm for light of wavelength 6000 Å. Find the radius of curvature of the lens and the film thickness.

Why: The recurring numerical set. Grating: d(&theta;) = n&lambda;. Newton's: D<sub>n</sub>&sup2; = 4n&lambda;R. Both have appeared verbatim with these numbers. Prep: unit2/02-newtons-rings.html (not built yet)

Q8 · any · 14 (7 x 2) marks

(a) Short note 1: Mach-Zehnder interferometer.

(b) Short note 2: properties of laser light / division of amplitude and wavefront.

Why: The short-notes question is unit-flexible and offers a fixed rotating pool. Mach-Zehnder is a Unit 2 item and appears in the pool often, so it is a safe pick in a Units 1+2 paper. Prep: unit2/04-mach-zehnder.html (not built yet)

The single highest-value fact on this page

Heisenberg's uncertainty principle, the Schrödinger equation and Newton's rings each appeared in 6 of 7 papers — the first two in Unit 1, the third in Unit 2, so all three are in your mid-sem. Optical-fibre numerical aperture appeared in 6 of 7 as well. Four stems cover the overwhelming majority of the questions you can actually be asked on the 13th.

Two facts to be careful about

Traps in the data
  • The short-notes question is unit-flexible. In almost every paper the final question offers three to five options drawn from two to four units and you pick any two. It is excluded from the unit counts, which means the real distribution varies more than the table shows — and that you can never be examined on an unpopular unit through it.
  • Older papers are not a good proxy. BT-201's paper format changed in 2022 and this matters. Nov-2019 prints 10 + 4 per question with a short-notes question of “any four”. From Jun-2020 onward it is 7 + 7 with “any two”. A 10-mark part expects a full derivation; a 7-mark part expects a focused explanation. If you revise using a 2019 paper you will over-prepare derivations for marks that now want structure. Use the GS papers from Jun-2022 onward. Also note the silences: the photoelectric effect, de Broglie wavelength, Davisson-Germer, Born interpretation, Fresnel biprism, Fabry-Perot and laser speckles were never asked between 2019 and 2025, despite all being on the syllabus. They are not the place to spend your time.

Section 5 · Timed mock

90-minute closed-book run

Set the timer, then close every other tab. Answer on paper. The point of the mock is not the score — it is finding out whether you can write full answers and still have ten minutes to check.

90:00 90 minutes — matches your 12:00–1:30 slot at 50 minutes/question if the paper is 8 printed.
The mock, as you will actually face it

Attempt any five of the eight. All questions equal marks. (a) 7 + (b) 7.

  1. 2 min read the whole paper once. Rank the eight questions easiest-to-hardest. Do not start writing yet.
  2. 5 min per (a) part, 9 min per (b) part. Fourteen marks in fourteen minutes is the real per-question budget, and it is why questions get abandoned.
  3. Definition first, always. One line before any diagram. A guaranteed mark for fifteen seconds.
  4. Diagram or table before prose. Examiners scan visuals. A labelled diagram is half the answer and it is the part you cannot write badly.
  5. Underline the command word (define, explain, differentiate, classify, derive, list) and box the final answer.
  6. Never leave a question blank. A definition plus a labelled diagram is about 40% of any question, and it is free.
  7. Reserve the last 10 minutes to add the closing significance line to every answer. That line is where the last mark per question lives.

Mark it honestly

Mark your own paper against the marking structure in each topic file's Questionnaire. If you scored under 12/20 on a question, you did not learn it — read the file, then re-run the mock in three days.

Hall tactics that actually move the score

  • Read the command word and obey it. “Differentiate” wants a table with four rows. “Explain” wants prose plus a diagram. All-prose for a differentiate question is the most common way to lose a full mark.
  • Answer the asked question, not the topic. If it asks for applications, the mark is in the application, not the definition.
  • Two-column tables beat paragraphs. A comparison scored on four pairs is four marks; the same four facts in prose often scores two.
  • Know more than the count they ask for. RGPV asks for “any four” or “any two”. Choose the ones you can define.
  • Write the units and the formula even when you cannot finish. A correctly written formula with a substituted value earns method marks that a blank line does not.
  • Leave a mark of space between questions. Examiners mark sequentially; a cramped boundary invites marks to migrate to the wrong answer.
Method, sources and confidence

How this page was built. Seven real RGPV BT-201 papers were downloaded from rgpvonline.com and read; every sub-question was classified into one of the five syllabus units. 119 sub-questions were classified. Unit weights and the repeat list are counts from that corpus. The recurring numericals were extracted with their givens, because RGPV re-uses not just the questions but the numbers.

Papers read: Nov-2019, Jun-2020 and Dec-2020 have real text layers; Jun-2022, Nov-2022, Jun-2023, Dec-2023 are OCR-derived from scans. Dec-2024 and Dec-2025 were checked for recency and confirm the ranking.

Syllabus source: RGPV Notes &mdash; BT-201 Engineering Physics (full text of all 5 units) — full unit text for all five units, cross-checked against the RGPV AICTE flexible-curricula scheme of July 2018.

Marks-format source: RGPV Ordinance 04(A) for B.E. (CBGS) §5.3–5.5 and the New Scheme of Examination W.E.F. July 2018, both from rgpv.ac.in.

What is confirmed vs inferred on this page. The 20-mark mid-sem bucket, the 70/20/10 split, the “attempt any five, equal marks” end-sem format, and the 14-marks-per-question split all come from official RGPV documents or real scanned papers. The paper pattern for the mid-sem, the exact MST-1 mark value, and the duration are inferred — RGPV publishes no mid-sem papers and defers the detail to each Board of Studies. Treat the predicted questions as high-probability, not certain.
