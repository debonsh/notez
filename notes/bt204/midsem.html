Section 1 · What will actually be on the paper

Format, and the assumption that keeps getting made

Read this before you plan the paper

Every RGPV first-year end-sem paper says the same three things: “Attempt any five questions. All questions carry equal marks. In case of any doubt the English version is final.” It prints 8 questions for 70 marks, so each is 14 marks, normally (a) 7 + (b) 7. There is no Section A / Section B and no compulsory-versus-choice split.

Several exam-prep pages claim RGPV papers run as “Section A: 5 × 2 marks, Section B: 2 × 5 marks”. That format is not in any RGPV paper. It was checked against 10 real scans across eight sessions (Nov-2019 to Dec-2025).

What is actually known about the mid-sem itself

Confirmed from the official RGPV ordinance and the July 2018 scheme: the whole semester mid-sem bucket is 20 marks per theory subject (70 end-sem + 20 mid-sem + 10 internal = 100), and RGPV requires at least two mid-term tests. So MST-1 is most likely 10 marks.

Two things nobody can confirm, because RGPV publishes no mid-sem papers and the Board of Studies sets the detail locally: whether MST-1 is 10 or 20 marks, and the paper's duration and section layout.

One real data point survives: an ITM Gwalior BT-204 Mid Term 1 paper circulates online and its instruction line reads “Attempt all questions.” No choice at all — a small question count, all compulsory, equal marks, which is the end-sem pattern scaled down.

How to plan for a paper nobody has seen

Do not memorise a layout. Memorise the topics — they transfer between any layout. Then when the paper lands, read the marks against each question and spend your time proportionally. The predicted paper below is written in the end-sem format because that is the house style RGPV reuses.

(a) Theodolite — explain its individual components with a labelled sketch.

(b) Explain the various levelling operations: profile, cross-sectional, differential and fly levelling.

Q6 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) The following consecutive staff readings were taken with a level at 20 m intervals: [12 readings]. The instrument was shifted after the 4th and 8th. The last reading is on a BM of RL 110.200 m. Find all RLs by the Rise and Fall method and give the arithmetic check.

(b) Explain the difference between the height of collimation method and the rise and fall method.

Why: The Rise and Fall levelling-book numerical appeared in 4 of 10 papers with almost identical wording. Practise this one numerical properly and it is 8 marks. Prep: unit2/05-levelling-rise-fall.html (not built yet)

Q7 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Explain the errors in chain surveying, and the concepts of local attraction, fore sight and back sight.

(b) Explain the procedure for traversing by the included-angle method and by the direct-angle method.

Why: Errors in chain surveying and traversing methods both recur in the Nov-2022 to Jun-2025 papers. Write the check column &mdash; sum of included angles = (2n &minus; 4) right angles. Prep: unit2/04-chaining-traversing.html (not built yet)

(a) Short note 1: composition of cement and its setting time.

(b) Short note 2: types of foundation, shallow and deep.

Why: The short-notes pool is unit-flexible and cement and foundations are the Unit 1 items that recur in it most. Prep: unit1/03-bricks-cement.html (not built yet)

1Workability of concreteThe property that determines how easily fresh concrete can be mixed, placed, compacted and finished without segregation or bleedingFactors: water content, aggregate shape and grading, cement content, admixtures, time since mixing. Naming the effect of each is the mark
2Nominal mix of concreteBy volume 1:2:4 (M15), 1:1.5:3 (M20), 1:1:2 (M25), against a design mix computed from w/c ratioA nominal mix is fixed by volume and needs no design; saying that distinction is the difference between a 5 and a 7
3Rise and Fall methodRise or fall = next staff reading − previous; RL = RL of first point + cumulative sumAlways give the arithmetic check: last RL − first RL must equal the sum of all rises and falls
4Local attractionThe error by which the line of sight is not perpendicular to the staff at a station, so readings disagree with the true difference in levelDetected when fore sight and back sight on the same station differ. Fix it by equalising the readings
5Reciprocal levellingTwo staff positions, observations interchanged between them, taking the mean of the two differences in levelUsed only when the leveller cannot be sited conveniently midway — say that and the method is complete
6Contour lineAn imaginary line joining points of equal reduced level, never crossing itself in ordinary groundState the properties: closed, no crossing, constant interval, fewest points in a valley and the most on a spur
7Bow's notationMembers of a force system are labelled with letters in order of the force polygon for the first system, then the spaces for the secondSay it removes the need to redraw a figure for every resultant position — that is the point of it
8Simpson's rule areaA = d/3 [(y0+yn) + 4(y1+y3+…) + 2(y2+y4+…)]Requires an even number of strips or intervals. Stating that condition is a mark

Two facts to be careful about

Traps in the data
  • The short-notes question is unit-flexible. In almost every paper the final question offers three to five options drawn from two to four units and you pick any two. It is excluded from the unit counts, which means the real distribution varies more than the table shows — and that you can never be examined on an unpopular unit through it.
  • Older papers are not a good proxy. The Nov-2019 and Jun-2020 BT-204 papers are CBGS-era and are not a usable proxy. Every question in them is a definition plus one sub-topic — pure recall — and the two together contain only 15 items against 15–16 per GS paper. From Nov-2022 (GS) onward roughly 30–40% of every BT-204 paper is numerical: levelling books, truss method-of-joints, area and volume rules, friction, moments of inertia. If you revise from 2019–20 material you will prepare zero numericals for a paper that is mostly numerical. The Nov-2019 paper is in particular only 8 items, and Unit 5 got just 2 of them.

Section 5 · Timed mock

90-minute closed-book run

Set the timer, then close every other tab. Answer on paper. The point of the mock is not the score — it is finding out whether you can write full answers and still have ten minutes to check.

90:00 90 minutes — matches your 12:00–1:30 slot at 50 minutes/question if the paper is 8 printed.
The mock, as you will actually face it

Attempt any five of the eight. All questions equal marks. (a) 7 + (b) 7.

  1. 2 min read the whole paper once. Rank the eight questions easiest-to-hardest. Do not start writing yet.
  2. 5 min per (a) part, 9 min per (b) part. Fourteen marks in fourteen minutes is the real per-question budget, and it is why questions get abandoned.
  3. Definition first, always. One line before any diagram. A guaranteed mark for fifteen seconds.
  4. Diagram or table before prose. Examiners scan visuals. A labelled diagram is half the answer and it is the part you cannot write badly.
  5. Underline the command word (define, explain, differentiate, classify, derive, list) and box the final answer.
  6. Never leave a question blank. A definition plus a labelled diagram is about 40% of any question, and it is free.
  7. Reserve the last 10 minutes to add the closing significance line to every answer. That line is where the last mark per question lives.

Mark it honestly

Mark your own paper against the marking structure in each topic file's Questionnaire. If you scored under 12/20 on a question, you did not learn it — read the file, then re-run the mock in three days.

Hall tactics that actually move the score

  • Read the command word and obey it. “Differentiate” wants a table with four rows. “Explain” wants prose plus a diagram. All-prose for a differentiate question is the most common way to lose a full mark.
  • Answer the asked question, not the topic. If it asks for applications, the mark is in the application, not the definition.
  • Two-column tables beat paragraphs. A comparison scored on four pairs is four marks; the same four facts in prose often scores two.
  • Know more than the count they ask for. RGPV asks for “any four” or “any two”. Choose the ones you can define.
  • Write the units and the formula even when you cannot finish. A correctly written formula with a substituted value earns method marks that a blank line does not.
  • Leave a mark of space between questions. Examiners mark sequentially; a cramped boundary invites marks to migrate to the wrong answer.

What is confirmed vs inferred on this page. The 20-mark mid-sem bucket, the 70/20/10 split, the “attempt any five, equal marks” end-sem format, and the 14-marks-per-question split all come from official RGPV documents or real scanned papers. Treat the predicted questions as high-probability, not certain.
