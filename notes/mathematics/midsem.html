Section 1 · What will actually be on the paper

Format, and the assumption that keeps getting made

Read this before you plan the paper

Every RGPV first-year end-sem paper says the same three things: “Attempt any five questions. All questions carry equal marks. In case of any doubt the English version is final.” It prints 8 questions for 70 marks, so each is 14 marks, normally (a) 7 + (b) 7. There is no Section A / Section B and no compulsory-versus-choice split.

Several exam-prep pages claim RGPV papers run as “Section A: 5 × 2 marks, Section B: 2 × 5 marks”. That format is not in any RGPV paper. It was checked against 8 real scans across eight papers (May-2019 to Dec-2023).

What is actually known about the mid-sem itself

Confirmed from the official RGPV ordinance and the July 2018 scheme: the whole semester mid-sem bucket is 20 marks per theory subject (70 end-sem + 20 mid-sem + 10 internal = 100), and RGPV requires at least two mid-term tests. So MST-1 is most likely 10 marks.

Two things nobody can confirm, because RGPV publishes no mid-sem papers and the Board of Studies sets the detail locally: whether MST-1 is 10 or 20 marks, and the paper's duration and section layout.

One real data point survives: an ITM Gwalior BT-204 Mid Term 1 paper circulates online and its instruction line reads “Attempt all questions.” No choice at all — a small question count, all compulsory, equal marks, which is the end-sem pattern scaled down.

How to plan for a paper nobody has seen

Do not memorise a layout. Memorise the topics — they transfer between any layout. Then when the paper lands, read the marks against each question and spend your time proportionally. The predicted paper below is written in the end-sem format because that is the house style RGPV reuses.

Section 2 · Predicted paper

BT102 — Units 1+2 only, predicted

RGPV end-sem house format (8 printed, attempt any 5, 14 each). The Jun-2020 and Dec-2020 papers print <b>7</b> against every (a) and (b) part explicitly, so the 7+7 split is read off the paper, not assumed.
Q1 · U1 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) State Rolle's theorem and verify it for a given function on a stated interval.

(b) Expand log x in powers of (x−1) by Taylor's theorem and hence evaluate log 1.1 correct to four decimal places.

Why: Rolle 5 of 8 papers, the log expansion 6 of 8. Both are recall-and-substitute, and the log one is the most reliable numerical in Unit 1. Prep: unit1/01-rolles-theorem.html

Q2 · U1 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Verify Lagrange's mean value theorem for f(x) = 2x² − 7x + 10 on the interval [2, 5].

(b) Find the maximum and minimum of a function of two variables, showing the classification.

Why: Lagrange 5 of 8, maxima/minima 6 of 8. The same function f(x)=2x&sup2;&minus;7x+10 has appeared verbatim in more than one paper. Prep: unit1/02-lagranges-mvt.html

Q3 · U1 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Show that ∂u/∂x + ∂u/∂y + ∂u/∂z = 0 when u = f(y−z, z−x, x−y).

(b) Discuss the maximum or minimum of f(x, y) = x³ − 4xy + 2y².

Why: The cyclic-partials identity appeared in May-19 and Jun-20. The cubic max-min is an exact repeat &mdash; Nov-19 Q2b and Jun-20 Q4b. Prep: unit1/04-maxima-minima.html

Q4 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Change the order of integration in the given double integral and hence evaluate it.

(b) Evaluate a double integral over a specified region.

Why: Change of order in 5 of 8 papers, double/triple integrals in 6. Unit 2 is numerical almost throughout, so these are pure practice items. Prep: unit2/01-definite-integrals.html (not built yet)

Q5 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Evaluate the limit as n tends to infinity of the given sum, expressing it as a definite integral.

(b) Prove the Beta-Gamma relationship β(m, n) = (m−1)!(n−1)!/(m+n−2)!.

Why: Limit-of-a-sum in 3 papers, Beta-Gamma in 3. Dec-2020 asked the Beta-Gamma identity in its factorial form; write both forms. Prep: unit2/03-beta-gamma.html (not built yet)

Q6 · U1 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Expand log(1 + sin x) by Maclaurin's theorem, first four terms.

(b) Find the first six terms of the expansion of a given function in a Taylor series about the origin.

Why: Maclaurin/Taylor expansions are the most common Unit 1 numerical. Four to six terms, then stop &mdash; do not compute more than asked. Prep: unit1/03-taylors-maclaurins.html

Q7 · U2 · 7 + 7 marks

(a) Find the area lying between the given pair of curves.

(b) Evaluate the area of the region bounded by x = 0, y = 0 and the given curve.

Why: Applications of definite integrals to area. Two papers ask this almost exactly. Sketch the region first &mdash; the sketch is a mark. Prep: unit2/01-definite-integrals.html (not built yet)

Q8 · any · 14 (7 x 2) marks

(a) Short note 1: Lagrange multipliers.

(b) Short note 2: Taylor's theorem for a function of two variables.

Why: The short-notes question is unit-flexible in every paper. Both of these are Unit 1, so they are safe picks in a mid-sem restricted to Units 1+2. Prep: unit1/04-maxima-minima.html

The single highest-value fact on this page

Consistency of a system of linear equations appeared in 8 of 8 papers and Cauchy-Hamilton in 6, eigen values in 7 — but all three are Matrices, which is Unit 5 and therefore NOT in your mid-sem. For your actual scope the priorities are: Maxima and minima of two variables (6 of 8 papers), Taylor/Maclaurin expansion of log x (6 of 8), and Rolle's and Lagrange's theorems (5 of 8 each). Those are the mid-sem.

Two facts to be careful about

Traps in the data
  • The short-notes question is unit-flexible. In almost every paper the final question offers three to five options drawn from two to four units and you pick any two. It is excluded from the unit counts, which means the real distribution varies more than the table shows — and that you can never be examined on an unpopular unit through it.
  • Older papers are not a good proxy. Two cautions on this dataset. First, there are no 2021 papers anywhere in the archives — the sequence jumps Dec-2020 straight to Jun-2022 — so the frequency counts skip a year. Second, most of these papers are OCR-derived, and OCR on mathematical notation is unreliable: the English question wording is sound, but exponents, integrals and matrix entries may be misread. The question topics and the frequencies are trustworthy; the exact formulas should be checked against the PDFs if you intend to memorise a specific numerical.

Section 5 · Timed mock

90-minute closed-book run

Set the timer, then close every other tab. Answer on paper. The point of the mock is not the score — it is finding out whether you can write full answers and still have ten minutes to check.

90:00 90 minutes — matches your 12:00–1:30 slot at 50 minutes/question if the paper is 8 printed.
The mock, as you will actually face it

Attempt any five of the eight. All questions equal marks. (a) 7 + (b) 7.

  1. 2 min read the whole paper once. Rank the eight questions easiest-to-hardest. Do not start writing yet.
  2. 5 min per (a) part, 9 min per (b) part. Fourteen marks in fourteen minutes is the real per-question budget, and it is why questions get abandoned.
  3. Definition first, always. One line before any diagram. A guaranteed mark for fifteen seconds.
  4. Diagram or table before prose. Examiners scan visuals. A labelled diagram is half the answer and it is the part you cannot write badly.
  5. Underline the command word (define, explain, differentiate, classify, derive, list) and box the final answer.
  6. Never leave a question blank. A definition plus a labelled diagram is about 40% of any question, and it is free.
  7. Reserve the last 10 minutes to add the closing significance line to every answer. That line is where the last mark per question lives.

Mark it honestly

Mark your own paper against the marking structure in each topic file's Questionnaire. If you scored under 12/20 on a question, you did not learn it — read the file, then re-run the mock in three days.

Hall tactics that actually move the score

  • Read the command word and obey it. “Differentiate” wants a table with four rows. “Explain” wants prose plus a diagram. All-prose for a differentiate question is the most common way to lose a full mark.
  • Answer the asked question, not the topic. If it asks for applications, the mark is in the application, not the definition.
  • Two-column tables beat paragraphs. A comparison scored on four pairs is four marks; the same four facts in prose often scores two.
  • Know more than the count they ask for. RGPV asks for “any four” or “any two”. Choose the ones you can define.
  • Write the units and the formula even when you cannot finish. A correctly written formula with a substituted value earns method marks that a blank line does not.
  • Leave a mark of space between questions. Examiners mark sequentially; a cramped boundary invites marks to migrate to the wrong answer.

What is confirmed vs inferred on this page. The 20-mark mid-sem bucket, the 70/20/10 split, the “attempt any five, equal marks” end-sem format, and the 14-marks-per-question split all come from official RGPV documents or real scanned papers.
