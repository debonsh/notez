Lesson · BT203 Unit 1 — Materials

Hooke's Law and Modulus of Elasticity — The Straight Line Every Material Starts With

Hooke's law is one sentence long and every calculation in this topic is that sentence with different words swapped in. The only thing that catches people is units: modulus is quoted in GPa while stress is quoted in MPa, and mixing them puts your answer out by a factor of a thousand. Fix that one habit and this topic is free marks.

The one-sentence statement

Within the elastic limit, the stress produced in a body is directly
proportional to the strain produced. Hence stress / strain = a constant
for that material, and that constant is the modulus of elasticity.
   WITHIN THE ELASTIC LIMIT
   -------------------------------------------
   stress   sigma  is proportional to   strain  e

                   sigma = E . e
                   sigma / e = E  (constant for the material)
   -------------------------------------------

The five words within the elastic limit carry the marks. Outside that range the body is permanently deformed, the graph is no longer a straight line, and the ratio σ/e is no longer constant. An answer that omits the phrase is an answer that is only right inside one part of the curve.

The four derived forms

FormEquationUse it when you know
Definitionσ = E × eσ and e
Rearrangede = σ / Eσ and E
RearrangedE = σ / eσ and e
GeometricδL = P L0 / (A E)load, length, area and E

The fourth is the one that catches people out. Combine σ = P/A with e = δL / L0 in σ = E × e and the stress and strain both cancel their areas and lengths, giving:

   sigma = E . e
   P/A   = E . (dL / L0)

        P . L0
   dL = ---------
         A . E

which is the elongation formula every bar, rod and tie in structural work uses.

Three moduli, three different meanings

ModulusSymbolStrain it measuresTypical value
Young's modulus (longitudinal)EExtension under tensionSteel 200 GPa, aluminium 70 GPa
Bulk modulusKChange in volume under uniform pressureLiquids very large, gases small
Modulus of rigidity (shear)GShear deformation under twisting forceSteel about 80 GPa

When BT-203 says “modulus of elasticity” without qualification it means Young's modulus E, the tensile one. Bulk modulus is what gets asked when the question mentions liquid or gas, because that is the only one a fluid really has.

Stiffness versus flexibility

Two words that sound like synonyms and are opposites.

  LOW E  -> STRETCHES A LOT          HIGH E -> STRETCHES ALMOST NOT AT ALL
  (FLEXIBLE)                          (STIFF)
       |                                      |
       v                                      v
  +--------------+  +--------------+  +--------------+  +--------------+
  |    LEAD      |  |  ALUMINIUM   |  |    STEEL     |  |   DIAMOND   |
  |  ~ 15 GPa   |  |  ~ 70 GPa    |  | ~ 200 GPa    |  |  ~ 1000 GPa |
  +--------------+  +--------------+  +--------------+  +--------------+
        flexible          moderate           stiff           very stiff
        use: seals         use: aircraft      use: structure  use: cutting

Stiffness is a property of the material and is measured by E. A stiff material has a large E, deforms very little under a given stress, and is described as hard or rigid — but note that hardness and stiffness are not the same thing, and neither is strength.

Flexibility is the ability of a structure to bend. A long slender beam can be made of very stiff steel and still be highly flexible, because flexibility depends on shape and section as well as on E. That distinction is worth a mark: stiffness is a property of the material, flexibility of the member.

Five-line answer format — every elasticity numeral 
Given:  load P, diameter d, length L0, modulus E
Find:   stress, strain, elongation, or E itself
Formula:A = pi/4 d^2 ; sigma = P/A ; e = sigma/E ; dL = e L0
Solve:  ALWAYS convert E and sigma to the same units first
Check:  the strain must be a pure number; the elongation must be small
The conversion that decides the question. 1 GPa = 1000 MPa = 1000 N/mm2. Either convert E to N/mm2, or convert the stress to GPa. Mixing MPa with GPa divides instead of multiplying and every answer comes out a thousand times too small. Everything else on this page is easier than that one conversion.

Solved A — finding the modulus

A steel bar is stressed to 250 MPa and its strain at that point is measured as 0.0005. Calculate Young's modulus for the material.

Given: σ = 250 MPa, e = 0.0005. Find: E. Formula: E = σ / e. Solve:

E = 250 / 0.0005 N/mm^2
  = 500 000 N/mm^2
  = 500 GPa

Answer: E = 500,000 N/mm2 = 500 GPa.

Check: this is far above the 200 GPa of steel, so it is not a steel — the figures correspond to a tungsten-class material, and the calculation is self-consistent. Structurally: 0.0005 strain is 0.5 mm on a 1 m bar, a small elastic deformation as expected. And dividing 250 by 0.0005 is the same as multiplying 250 by 2000, which is how the modulus of a hard metal arrives at a figure five times that of steel. Nothing in the arithmetic forces an error.

Solved B — strain from stress and modulus

A steel tie-rod of 25 mm diameter carries an axial load of 40 kN. Taking E for steel as 200 GPa, calculate the stress and the strain in the rod.

Given: d = 25 mm, P = 40 kN = 40,000 N, E = 200 GPa = 200,000 N/mm2. Find: σ, e. Formula: σ = P/A, then e = σ/E. Solve:

A     = (pi / 4) x 25^2 = 490.874 mm^2
sigma = 40000 / 490.874 = 81.49 N/mm^2 = 81.49 MPa

e = 81.49 / 200000 = 0.0004074   (dimensionless)

Answer: stress 81.49 MPa, strain 0.000407.

Check: the units cancel in the strain step, leaving a pure number as a strain must be. Second check: 0.000407 strain on a 2 m rod gives 0.8149 mm of elongation, and 2000 mm × 0.0004074 = 0.8149 mm — a fraction of a millimetre on a two-metre rod, exactly the order of magnitude a 40 kN load should produce. Had E been left in GPa while the stress was in MPa, the strain would have come out 0.407, i.e. 40% strain, which is absurd for a steel tie and would have been the tell.

Solved C — elongation of a bar

How much does a 2 m long steel bar of 25 mm diameter stretch under the same 40 kN load? Take E = 200 GPa.

Same data as Solved B, different question — which is the point: the stress and strain are intermediate quantities, not the answer.

Given: P = 40,000 N, L0 = 2 m = 2000 mm, A = 490.874 mm2, E = 200,000 N/mm2. Find: δL. Formula: δL = P L0 / (A E). Solve:

dL = P L0 / (A E)
   = 40000 x 2000 / (490.874 x 200000)
   = 80 000 000 / 98 174 800
   = 0.8149 mm

   or  dL = e L0 = 0.0004074 x 2000 = 0.8149 mm

Answer: the bar stretches 0.815 mm.

Check: the two routes agree to four figures — 0.8149 mm from the direct formula and from e × L0 — because the direct formula was derived from the two together, so they are not independent. A genuine independent check is dimensional: P L0 is N × mm and A E is mm2 × N/mm2, which is also N × mm, so the quotient must be mm — and it is. As a physical sanity check, 0.815 mm on 2000 mm is about 1 part in 2455, i.e. a strain near 4.07 × 10−4, which agrees with the strain computed independently in Solved B.

Numeral traps in this topic
  • MPa against GPa. Mixing them is the single most common error in this topic. 1 GPa = 1000 MPa = 1000 N/mm2.
  • Hooke's law holds only in the elastic range. A strain of 0.4 is outside it; if a question gives a strain above about 0.005 for a metal, the linear law does not apply.
  • Strain is dimensionless. If your answer carries units, the modulus was in the wrong units.
  • δL is not L. The elongation is a fraction of the length; writing the new length instead of the increase loses the mark, and the increase is usually a fraction of a millimetre.
  • Length must be in the same unit as the answer. Mixing 2 m with a diameter in mm is the second commonest slip, immediately after the GPa problem.
Special lines for extra marks
  • Opening line — “Within the elastic limit, the stress produced in a body is directly proportional to the strain produced, so the ratio σ/e is constant for that material and is the modulus of elasticity.”
  • Significance line — “The slope of the linear portion of the stress-strain curve is E, so the slope of a material's elastic behaviour is a single number identifying it as surely as its density.”
  • Method line — “Always bring stress and modulus to the same units first — 1 GPa = 1000 MPa = 1000 N/mm2 — because strain is their ratio and is therefore dimensionless.”
  • Limitation line — “The law is empirical and is valid only inside the elastic limit; beyond it the material deforms permanently and σ/e is no longer constant.”
  • Closing line — “Hence E = σ/e is the constant of proportionality, and the elongation of any bar is δL = P L0 / (A E).”
Exam tips
  • Write the statement first, with the phrase "within the elastic limit" — it is a mark, and it is the mark most often left out.
  • Convert units before you substitute — write the conversion line on the page so the examiner sees it.
  • Write all four forms of the equation — it covers whichever one the question wants.
  • Underline the keyword — elastic limit, proportional, modulus, dimensionless.
  • State what the answer means — "the bar stretches 0.815 mm" scores better than a bare number.
  • Attempt order — do this question early; the arithmetic is short and certain.

Hooke's Law and Modulus of Elasticity — Full Revision

Statement to write verbatim

Within the elastic limit, the stress produced in a body is directly
proportional to the strain produced. Hence stress / strain = a constant
for that material, and that constant is the modulus of elasticity.

   sigma = E . e

Derivation in four steps

1. Start from        sigma = E . e
2. Substitute stress sigma = P/A  and strain  e = dL / L0
       P/A = E . (dL / L0)
3. Rearrange for the elongation of a bar
       P . L0
  dL = ---------
        A . E
4. Recover the other two forms
  e = sigma / E        E = sigma / e

Reference table

QuantityFormulaUnit

Longitudinal straine = σ / Edimensionless

ElongationδL = P L0 / (A E)mm

Definitions to reproduce

  • Stress — internal resisting force per unit area, σ = P / A.
  • Strain — change in dimension per unit original dimension, e = δL / L0; dimensionless.
  • Elastic limit — the stress beyond which the body does not return to its original dimensions.
  • Modulus of elasticity (Young's modulus) — the constant of proportionality between stress and longitudinal strain within the elastic limit.
  • Stiffness — resistance to deformation, a property of the material measured by E.
  • Flexibility — the ability of a member to bend, depending on the material and on its shape, length and section.

Checklist before you call the topic done

  • [ ] Can state Hooke's law including "within the elastic limit"
  • [ ] Can write all four forms, including δL = P L0/(A E)
  • [ ] Can say where E comes off the stress-strain curve — the slope of the linear part
  • [ ] Can distinguish stiffness from flexibility, material versus member
  • [ ] Can convert 200 GPa to 200,000 N/mm2 without hesitating
  • [ ] Can state E for steel, aluminium and one more material

Numeral shortcut

Four moves, always in this order. (1) A = π/4 d2. (2) σ = P / A, in MPa. (3) Convert E to N/mm2 — 200 GPa = 200,000 N/mm2. (4) e = σ / E must come out dimensionless, then δL = e L0 with L0 in the unit you want the answer in. If the strain has units, step 3 was skipped.

Model answer · 7 marks

State and explain Hooke's law. Define the modulus of elasticity and explain how it is determined.

Write the statement with its qualification, then the equation block, then derive rather than quote the elongation formula.

        WITHIN THE ELASTIC LIMIT
        -------------------------------------------
        stress   sigma  is proportional to   strain  e

                        sigma = E . e
                        sigma / e = E  (constant for the material)
        -------------------------------------------

        FOUR FORMS OF THE SAME LAW

        sigma = E . e           e = sigma / E
        E = sigma / e           dL = P L0 / (A E)

        DERIVATION OF THE ELONGATION FORM

        sigma = E . e
        P/A   = E . (dL / L0)

              P . L0
        dL = ---------
              A . E

        ---------------------------------------------------------------
        UNITS:  1 GPa = 1000 MPa = 1000 N/mm2   <-- convert FIRST
        ---------------------------------------------------------------

        STRESS
          |          /
          |        / |         the straight-line portion
          |      /   |         of the curve is the ELASTIC region
          |    /     |         its SLOPE is Young's modulus
          |  /       |
          |/         |
          +-----------+----------------> STRAIN
          |    e
          |
          slope = stress / strain = E
  1. Statement: within the elastic limit, the stress produced in a body is directly proportional to the strain produced.
  2. Modulus of elasticity: the constant of proportionality between stress and longitudinal strain, E = σ / e, measured in N/mm2 or GPa.
  3. Other forms: σ = E × e, and e = σ / E.
  4. Derivation of the elongation form: substituting σ = P/A and e = δL / L0 into σ = E × e gives P/A = E δL / L0, hence δL = P L0 / (A E).
  5. Determination: E is obtained as the slope of the straight-line proportional portion of the stress-strain curve, or by loading a specimen and dividing a measured stress by the corresponding strain.
  6. Limitation: the law is empirical and valid only inside the elastic limit; beyond it the body deforms permanently and the ratio is no longer constant.

Exam close: "Hence E = σ/e is the constant of proportionality and the elongation of any bar is δL = P L0/(A E), with 1 GPa = 1000 MPa always converted before substituting."

Model answer · 5 marks

Differentiate between stiffness and flexibility. Is stiffness a property of the material or of the member?

Define both, then give the verdict, then prove the verdict with the long slender beam. The example is what carries the distinction.

   STIFFNESS  -- a property of the MATERIAL
   set by Young's modulus E
   low E  <--------------------------> high E
   lead 15 GPa   aluminium 70   steel 200   diamond 1000
   stretches a lot            stretches almost nothing

   FLEXIBILITY -- a property of the MEMBER
   set by shape, length and section as well as by E

        LONG SLENDER BEAM (steel, E = 200 GPa - very stiff material)
        |<---------------------- long ---------------------->|
        +----------------------------------------------------+
          bends a LOT  ->  the MEMBER is flexible

        SHORT STUBBY BLOCK of the SAME steel
        +----------+
          bends very little -> the MEMBER is stiff

   same E, very different flexibility

DefinitionResistance to deformation under loadAbility of a member to bend

Model answer · 5 marks

A 2 m long, 25 mm diameter steel bar stretches 0.815 mm under 40 kN. Find its strain and verify Young's modulus.

Work forward to the stress, then take the strain from the measured elongation, then run Hooke's law backwards to recover E.

   GIVEN
     d0   = 25 mm            L0  = 2000 mm
     P    = 40 kN            dL  = 0.815 mm

   STEP 1  area
     A = (pi / 4) x 25^2 = 490.874 mm^2

   STEP 2  stress
     sigma = 40000 / 490.874 = 81.49 MPa

   STEP 3  strain from the measured extension
     e = dL / L0 = 0.815 / 2000 = 0.0004075

   STEP 4  run the law BACKWARDS
     E = sigma / e = 81.49 / 0.0004075
       = 200 000 N/mm^2
       = 200 GPA  <-- exactly the handbook value for steel

   ---------------------------------------------------------------
   VERIFIED.  If this had come out as 0.2 GPa or 200 000 GPa,
   the error would have been MPa mixed with GPa.
   ---------------------------------------------------------------

A     = (pi/4) x 25^2      = 490.874 mm^2
sigma = 40000 / 490.874      = 81.49 MPa
e     = 0.815 / 2000         = 0.0004075
E     = 81.49 / 0.0004075    = 200 000 N/mm^2 = 200 GPa

Answer: the strain is 0.0004075 and the recovered modulus is 200 GPa, which matches the accepted value for steel and therefore verifies both the measurement and the law.

Exam close: "Since the modulus recovered from the extension is exactly 200 GPa, the bar is steel and the whole set of readings is internally consistent; a result a factor of a thousand away would indicate that MPa and GPa had been mixed."
